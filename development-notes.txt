HOW I APPROACHED IT

I organized the work around getting one useful profile from a website:
collect the pages, structure the information, let the owner edit and save,
then use that profile to build marketing instructions. Testing different
businesses helped show where the extraction rules needed more work.

The main decisions were about what to collect, what to trust, and how much
work to leave to the owner.

WHY I MADE THESE CHOICES

- Start with what the website already provides. I wanted an owner to check
  a useful draft instead of starting with a long form. Missing details
  should not stop them from saving.

- Separate business information from marketing choices. A website might
  list a service that the owner does not want to promote right now. That
  is why offering priorities and pricing preferences are separate controls.

- Keep evidence and approval separate. A source shows where something came
  from. It does not mean the owner wants to use it in marketing, especially
  when prices, guarantees, or certifications are involved.

- Reuse information instead of creating extra steps. Customer questions
  already exist in the FAQ records. The decision map uses those records,
  so editing an answer also changes what the brief can use.

- Keep the crawl limited. Reading an entire website would take longer and
  could collect more unrelated content. The current limit is the starting
  page plus six related-page attempts. This helps control waiting time,
  but it can also leave useful information undiscovered.

- Check improvements against different business types. A rule that removes
  a product-option heading can also remove a real service name. For example,
  Color is a valid salon service. Tests need to check both false matches
  and legitimate information that should remain.

WHAT I LEARNED AND WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT

I learned how one data structure can connect several parts of an app.
Keeping the editor, saved profile, and briefs connected makes changes
more manageable. I also learned that TypeScript checks the expected shape
of data, while downloaded content and saved records still need runtime
checks. My next step would be improving extraction accuracy using more varied
examples, especially for people and statements that can have different
meanings depending on context.
